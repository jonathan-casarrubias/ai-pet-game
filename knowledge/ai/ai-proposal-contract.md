# AI Proposal Contract

This document defines the conceptual contract between Game Core and bounded AI capabilities. The contract allows AI to enrich narrative and supported gameplay variation without making AI the source of game rules, authoritative state, progression, rewards, or safety decisions.

## Purpose and boundary

The contract separates creative generation from authoritative gameplay:

```text
Game Core identifies an allowed capability
        ↓
Controlled context
        ↓
AI capability
        ↓
Structured proposal
        ↓
Structural validation
        ↓
Semantic validation
        ↓
Safety validation
        ↓
Game Core domain validation
        ↓
Accepted outcome or safe fallback
        ↓
Authoritative state transition
```

The contract applies only to bounded AI uses explicitly supported by the game. It is not a general conversation protocol, a mechanism for giving AI game authority, or a substitute for Game Core rules. AI may propose creative content or bounded variations; Game Core decides what the game accepts and what actually happens.

## Distinct contract concepts

The following concepts must not be treated as interchangeable:

- **Controlled context:** The limited, capability-specific information selected by Game Core and the application boundary for one AI invocation.
- **AI proposal:** The structured, untrusted response generated by the AI capability within that context.
- **Authoritative game decision:** Game Core's validated determination of whether the proposal is allowed and what domain outcome follows.
- **Persisted state:** The accepted authoritative result stored through the persistence boundary. Persistence records the result; it does not decide its meaning or validity.

Player intent is a separate input. The client supplies intent, the application transports it, and Game Core determines whether the interaction is valid and whether AI is needed. AI does not receive authority merely because it is responding to a player request.

## Controlled context

Controlled context is the smallest sufficient domain view needed for one allowed capability. It is selected before invocation and must not depend on the AI discovering additional information by querying the game, database, or external services.

Depending on the capability, context may include:

- The current bounded narrative or gameplay situation.
- Relevant game-world facts and allowed constraints.
- Selected pet identity or personality information.
- Relevant structured game memory or progression.
- Current adventure, quest, or mini-game context when applicable.
- The permitted purpose and expected kind of proposal.
- Presentation limits or other capability-specific boundaries.

Controlled context is not:

- Unrestricted game state or direct database access.
- Unrestricted raw conversation history.
- Secrets, credentials, infrastructure handles, or provider internals.
- Unnecessary player or child information.
- Permission to browse or retrieve unrestricted external information during normal gameplay.

Game Core remains responsible for deciding what context is safe and relevant. The AI must not infer that omitted information is available or use the proposal to request access to it.

## Identifying an AI capability

An AI capability is a named, bounded purpose for using AI. Conceptually, it identifies:

- What kind of creative contribution is allowed.
- What controlled context may be provided.
- What proposal category may be returned.
- What the capability explicitly cannot produce.
- Which validation rules and safety constraints apply.
- What safe behavior is available when generation fails or is rejected.

The capability boundary prevents the system from treating every player question as unrestricted chat. A capability may support pet dialogue, narrative variation, a variation within an existing adventure or quest concept, a bounded mini-game content variation, or another explicitly authorized creative use. The exact capabilities remain a feature-design concern.

Game Core or the application boundary identifies the capability. The AI does not select its own authority, expand the capability, or turn an allowed request into an unrelated game operation.

## Conceptual proposal categories

AI proposals may belong to categories such as:

- **Pet dialogue:** Bounded, child-appropriate expression for the current interaction.
- **Narrative variation:** A creative presentation of an already supported game situation.
- **Adventure or quest variation:** A bounded variation within a predefined game concept.
- **Mini-game variation:** Permitted content or presentation variation within an existing mini-game concept.
- **Other explicitly supported creative content:** A future category only when Game Core defines its purpose, boundaries, validation, and fallback behavior.

These categories describe creative scope, not authority. A proposal cannot create a new rule, reward, progression path, quest type, mini-game, or state transition merely by placing it in a recognized category.

## Required properties of a structured proposal

Gameplay-affecting AI output must be structured rather than free-form text interpreted as a command. Without defining a concrete schema, every proposal conceptually needs to be:

- **Capability-specific:** It clearly belongs to the capability that requested it.
- **Explicit in meaning:** Its intended creative contribution is understandable without guessing at hidden commands.
- **Bounded:** It uses only content, choices, and values allowed by the capability and current game-world boundaries.
- **Complete enough to evaluate:** It contains the information required for the applicable validation layers, or is rejected as incomplete.
- **Unambiguous:** It does not rely on implicit instructions, arbitrary fields, or provider-specific behavior to determine gameplay.
- **Safe to inspect:** It can be evaluated without executing model-generated actions or granting model-generated effects.
- **Independent of provider details:** Its domain meaning does not depend on a particular model, provider, SDK, or prompt format.
- **Suitable for deterministic interpretation:** Game Core can decide whether to accept it without treating creative wording as an authoritative rule.

A structured proposal remains untrusted. Structure helps the system inspect the response; it does not make the response correct, safe, or valid.

## Validation layers

All proposals pass through layered validation. A proposal that fails any required layer cannot directly affect authoritative state.

### 1. Structural or schema validation

The integration first determines whether the response can be read as the expected proposal shape for the identified capability. This includes confirming that the proposal is present, recognizable, uses permitted categories and fields, and respects the applicable basic limits.

Structural validity only means that the response can be inspected. It does not mean the proposal is appropriate or allowed by the game.

### 2. Semantic validation

Semantic validation determines whether the proposal makes sense for its capability and controlled context. It checks that the response is relevant, coherent, bounded, and consistent with the current narrative or gameplay situation.

Semantic validation rejects proposals that are structurally valid but off-topic, contradictory, incomplete in meaning, outside the requested capability, or attempting to introduce unsupported content.

### 3. Safety validation

Safety validation checks whether the proposal is appropriate for a child-oriented bounded game. It must reject content that encourages unsafe behavior, secrecy, emotional dependency, manipulation, unnecessary disclosure of personal information, unrestricted external access, or other prohibited behavior.

Safety validation also checks that creative content does not weaken the game's boundaries or introduce unsafe or undefined gameplay through AI generation. Prompt instructions alone are not sufficient; safety is reinforced by limited context, structured output, validation, and fallback behavior.

### 4. Game Core domain validation

Game Core performs the final authoritative validation against current game state and rules. It determines whether the proposal is allowed for the current capability, compatible with current adventure or interaction state, and consistent with valid transitions, progression, rewards, personality behavior, discoveries, and other applicable domain constraints.

Only Game Core can accept a proposal as a contributor to an authoritative outcome. A proposal may pass structural, semantic, and safety validation and still be rejected because it does not fit the current state or rules.

## Acceptance, rejection, and fallback

### Accepted proposal

An accepted proposal is still not itself authoritative state. It is an approved input to a Game Core decision. Game Core determines the actual outcome and state transition, and only that accepted domain result may be persisted or presented as an applied gameplay result.

### Rejected proposal

A rejected proposal contributes nothing to authoritative state. Game Core may return a safe bounded result, use a predefined or deterministic alternative, or leave the state unchanged according to the capability's designed behavior.

The system must not present a rejected proposal as though it was accepted, grant its suggested reward, or apply its suggested progression, personality change, discovery, or event.

### Failed generation or unavailable AI

AI may be unavailable, time out, return an empty response, return malformed output, or fail any validation layer. These are normal boundary conditions. They must not authorize a bypass of Game Core rules.

When a capability supports it, the game may use deterministic narrative, predefined content, or another safe bounded fallback. If no suitable fallback exists, the game may return a safe unavailable result and leave authoritative state unchanged. Valid non-AI gameplay must remain possible where the feature supports a non-AI path.

No failed or rejected proposal may cause partial state mutation. In particular, it must not partially apply rewards, progression, inventory, personality changes, discoveries, adventure completion, or domain events.

## AI is always untrusted

All AI output is untrusted input, even when it is:

- Structurally valid.
- Semantically coherent.
- Child-appropriate in isolation.
- Consistent with the requested capability.
- Generated by a known provider or model.
- Previously accepted in a similar situation.

The model cannot execute game actions, mutate state, grant rewards, define rules, or access persistence directly. Free-form text must never be interpreted as an implicit command, and structured content must never be treated as authoritative merely because it appears plausible.

## Responsibilities by boundary

### Game Core

Game Core:

- Defines the game capabilities and domain boundaries that may use AI.
- Determines the controlled context required for a capability.
- Owns authoritative rules, state, validation, progression, rewards, personality behavior, and outcomes.
- Validates proposals against current state, domain rules, and safety constraints.
- Accepts, rejects, or safely disregards proposals.
- Determines the actual state transition and domain events.
- Remains valid when AI is absent, unavailable, or replaced.

Game Core never delegates an authoritative decision to the model.

### AI capability and integration boundary

The AI capability and integration boundary:

- Receives only the controlled context allowed for its capability.
- Produces a bounded structured proposal or reports failure.
- Handles provider/model invocation and response parsing outside Game Core.
- Treats its own output as untrusted.
- Does not access persistence or unrestricted game state.
- Does not directly mutate authoritative state or claim that a gameplay effect occurred.

The AI capability enriches the game; it does not define the game.

### API and application layer

The API/application layer:

- Receives and validates transport-level input.
- Coordinates the player interaction, Game Core, AI integration, and persistence boundaries.
- Supplies or obtains the controlled context through the established application flow.
- Maps domain outcomes and failures to client-safe responses.

The API transports and orchestrates. It does not create alternate gameplay rules, accept AI output without Game Core validation, or apply state changes on behalf of a failed domain decision.

### Persistence

Persistence stores and retrieves accepted authoritative game data. PostgreSQL hosted on Neon is the selected persistence mechanism, but persistence does not validate AI proposals, define game rules, grant rewards, or decide state transitions.

Only accepted Game Core results may be treated as authoritative persisted state. A successful write does not make an invalid proposal valid, and a stored value does not authorize a future transition without Game Core evaluation.

## Provider and model independence

The contract exists above any particular AI provider, model, local runtime, or SDK. Ollama may be used during local development, but it is not a Game Core dependency and is not part of the domain contract.

Changing a provider, model, or SDK may change creative expression, latency, or integration behavior. It must not change the authority of Game Core, the meaning of authoritative state, the validation responsibilities, or the requirement that AI output be treated as untrusted.

Provider-specific request and response behavior belongs outside Game Core. The integration boundary adapts that behavior to the conceptual proposal contract.

## Contract evolution and compatibility

The proposal contract must evolve without weakening authority boundaries. Conceptual evolution principles are:

- Capability meaning and allowed proposal categories remain explicit.
- Changes that alter the meaning of a proposal, its allowed effects, or its validation rules are treated as deliberate contract changes.
- Additive creative variation is preferred when it does not change authoritative interpretation.
- Game Core remains the source of truth when proposal expectations and model output disagree.
- Provider-specific changes are absorbed by the integration boundary where possible.
- Older, incomplete, or incompatible proposals are rejected or handled through a safe fallback rather than interpreted permissively.
- Validation and fallback behavior evolve with the capability, domain rules, and child-safety requirements.
- Persisted authoritative state is not rewritten merely because a provider or model changes its creative output.

The exact versioning mechanism is an implementation concern. The stable requirement is that contract evolution must be explicit, testable, and safe, with no silent expansion of AI authority.

## Conceptual guarantees

The contract guarantees that:

1. Game Core is the authoritative source of rules, state transitions, progression, rewards, and accepted outcomes.
2. AI proposes; Game Core decides.
3. Controlled context limits what an AI capability can use and expose.
4. Structured output is inspectable but never trusted by default.
5. Structural, semantic, safety, and domain validation remain distinct responsibilities.
6. Rejected, malformed, unsafe, unavailable, or domain-invalid AI output cannot partially mutate game state.
7. Persistence stores accepted results without becoming the source of game rules.
8. The API/application layer orchestrates transport and integration without defining gameplay.
9. Replacing the provider, model, local runtime, or SDK does not require changing Game Core authority.

This contract keeps the game functional and understandable independently of AI generation while preserving AI's role as a bounded narrative and creative engine.
