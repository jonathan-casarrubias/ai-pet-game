# Player Experience and Core Loop

This document defines the conceptual player experience of the AI Pet Game. It brings together the established pet, world, gameplay, memory, personality, progression, evolution, and bounded AI concepts without defining specific mechanics, screens, or implementation contracts.

## Product experience

The player should experience a recognizable virtual Pet living in a bounded game world. The Pet provides continuity and a friendly perspective as the player becomes curious, interacts, explores Adventures and Quests, participates in Mini-games or other supported activities, makes discoveries, and sees the Pet and world develop over time.

The intended experience combines:

- A persistent fictional Pet with a recognizable identity.
- Curiosity about the Pet and the game world.
- Interaction that leads toward supported play.
- Adventures, Quests, Mini-games, and other bounded activities.
- Meaningful discovery and incidental learning.
- Progression, accepted rewards, and Pet development.
- Structured memory of meaningful gameplay.
- Personality expression that responds to how the game has been played.

This is a game first and an AI experience second. The product is not primarily an AI chat service with a Pet avatar. Conversation, narrative, and generated content are valuable because they lead into or enrich a persistent game experience whose rules and outcomes are owned by Game Core.

## Core loop

The established core loop is:

```text
Curiosity
    → question or interaction
    → Pet or narrative response
    → Adventure or Quest
    → Mini-game, activity, or discovery
    → accepted outcome
    → progression, reward, personality, memory, or Pet evolution
    → new curiosity
```

Not every cycle contains every element. A short interaction may produce a bounded Pet response or discovery. Another interaction may expand into an Adventure, Quest, Mini-game, or other supported activity. The shape may vary, but the relationship between curiosity, meaningful play, accepted outcomes, and new possibilities should remain recognizable.

Game Core determines what actually happened at each authoritative step. AI may enrich expression or propose bounded variation, but it cannot create a mechanic, completion, reward, progression change, memory fact, or evolution independently. The loop must remain valid when AI is unavailable through predefined or deterministic behavior where the feature supports it.

## Curiosity

Curiosity is a core gameplay driver: an opportunity to notice, wonder, explore, ask, choose, or discover something within the bounded game world.

Curiosity may emerge from:

- The Pet's reactions and personality expression.
- The current game world and its supported possibilities.
- Previous discoveries and meaningful memory.
- New experiences made available through progression.
- An Adventure, Quest, Mini-game, or other activity.
- Unresolved or supported game-world possibilities.

Curiosity should lead toward a meaningful interaction or activity rather than becoming a reason for endless conversation. A question or expression of interest is player input. Game Core determines whether it maps to a supported interaction, narrative response, Adventure, Quest, discovery, or other outcome.

The exact ways curiosity is created, stored, prioritized, or refreshed remain open. They must preserve player agency and the bounded nature of the game.

## Interaction with the Pet

The Pet is:

- The central player-facing character.
- A guide through the fictional world.
- A companion within the game world, not an unrestricted real-world companion.
- A narrative interface for supported experiences.
- A source of prompts toward Adventures, Quests, Mini-games, discoveries, and other gameplay.

The Pet helps make the world feel continuous and responsive. It may react to accepted gameplay, frame a discovery, suggest a supported possibility, or celebrate an accomplishment. The Pet's behavior remains bounded by game rules, child-safety principles, structured memory, personality state, and the capabilities defined by Game Core.

The Pet must not imply emotional dependency, secrecy, exclusivity, or responsibility on the part of the child. The child does not owe the Pet loyalty, continued participation, emotional care, or protection from adults. A recognizable personality should support play without making the Pet appear helpless without the child.

## Conversation as a gameplay entry point

Conversation and interaction can be an entry point into gameplay rather than the primary product:

```text
Player asks or interacts
        ↓
Pet responds within the bounded world
        ↓
Response reveals or suggests a supported possibility
        ↓
Game Core evaluates the current state and intent
        ↓
Supported gameplay begins, continues, or remains unchanged
```

The response may be generated by AI, provided by predefined content, or produced through another supported path. A response can create interest without creating an authoritative game outcome. AI-generated narrative must not define new mechanics, promise an unapproved reward, complete a Quest, or change Pet state by itself.

## Adventures and Quests

Adventures and Quests transform curiosity into structured gameplay.

- **Adventure:** A broader bounded game-world experience combining narrative context, interaction, exploration, discovery, and supported gameplay.
- **Quest:** A bounded objective or supported unit of activity within an Adventure or another established game experience.

They give the player direction and continuity without reducing the experience to a task string or a conversation transcript. They also prevent the Pet from becoming the only source of content: the player is moving through a game world with supported objectives, activities, and outcomes.

Game Core determines when an Adventure or Quest is available, started, progressed, completed, failed, abandoned, or interrupted when those concepts are defined. AI may propose bounded variation within a supported concept, and the client may present it, but neither can establish authoritative completion or invent an unsupported structure.

## Mini-games and supported activities

Mini-games and other supported activities are actual gameplay, not decorative additions around generated dialogue. They give the player something meaningful to do, create opportunities for discovery and mastery, and provide valid results that may contribute to progression, rewards, memory, personality, or Pet evolution when the rules support those consequences.

The client presents the activity and collects input. Game Core owns the relevant domain rules and determines the accepted result. A local score, animation, or visual completion state is not authoritative until Game Core validates it.

The conceptual domain boundary remains independent of the presentation technology. A future client or game technology may present the same supported activity differently without moving its authoritative rules into the client. No specific Mini-games or mechanics are defined here.

## Discovery and incidental learning

Discovery and learning should emerge naturally from curiosity and play. The experience should feel contextual, curiosity-driven, age-appropriate, bounded, and meaningful rather than like a school lesson disguised as a game.

An Adventure, Quest, Mini-game, or interaction may help the player notice a relationship, make a connection, experiment, or understand something within the game world. The game should not become a homework system, classroom, generic educational chatbot, or unrestricted factual question-and-answer service.

AI may present or contextualize bounded content, but it is not an unrestricted factual authority. When factual content becomes important to gameplay, the architecture should allow future controlled knowledge sources or content boundaries without changing Game Core's authority over rules and outcomes. This document does not define such a knowledge architecture.

## Meaningful outcomes

Gameplay produces an accepted outcome only when Game Core validates the player's intent, current state, any applicable AI proposal, and the relevant rules. An accepted outcome may contribute to:

- Progression.
- A reward or optional inventory change.
- Personality-related state or expression.
- Structured game memory.
- Pet evolution.
- New available experiences.
- A meaningful domain event.

Not every action needs to produce every consequence. A result may be meaningful without granting a reward, and a presentation may celebrate an outcome without changing every area of state. Game Core decides which consequences are valid and which facts are worth persisting or remembering.

## Personality in the experience

Personality should be felt through the Pet's reactions, narrative style, supported experience variations, presentation, and future curiosity. It should make the Pet seem consistent and responsive across meaningful gameplay without becoming a psychological profile of the child.

Personality belongs to authoritative Game Core state and may evolve from accepted gameplay signals. It can influence how an already-valid experience is expressed or which supported possibility is offered, but it must not invent mechanics, force a play style, grant rewards, or tell the child what kind of person they are.

The player must be able to experiment and change how they play. Temporary choices should not create irreversible judgments, and the Pet's fictional personality must remain distinct from claims about the child's real-world identity or private feelings. Exact traits are intentionally undefined.

## Memory in the experience

Structured game memory creates continuity without making raw conversation history the product's memory model. The player may experience that:

- The Pet remembers meaningful discoveries.
- Previous Adventures and Quests matter.
- Past accomplishments influence future supported experiences.
- The world becomes more continuous over time.

Memory represents accepted gameplay facts, such as discoveries, completed experiences, progression, personality-related signals, and meaningful domain events when the rules recognize them. It is bounded, relevant, and selected for future gameplay or controlled AI context.

An AI statement or a client display does not create a memory fact. Game Core determines whether something happened, whether it is meaningful enough to remember, and how it may influence future decisions.

## Progression and Pet evolution in the experience

Progression should make the world gradually open up through meaningful play. It may reveal new supported experiences, Adventures, Quests, discoveries, Pet capabilities, bounded interactions, or other game-world possibilities. Exact levels, XP, currencies, and unlock mechanics are intentionally deferred.

Pet evolution should feel like a consequence of the player's journey. It may connect accepted progression with discoveries, personality, Adventures, Quests, Mini-games, memory, and new bounded experiences. It is not a random AI-generated story event or a client-side animation. Game Core decides whether an evolution occurred and which authoritative state changes follow.

The long-term promise is not endless content generated by a model. It is a Pet and world that develop through accumulated, meaningful gameplay.

## Player agency

The player should feel that they are:

- Choosing what to explore.
- Experimenting with supported possibilities.
- Making meaningful gameplay decisions.
- Discovering consequences.
- Shaping the journey through play.

The game should not make the player feel psychologically analyzed or assigned a fixed identity. It must avoid forced play styles, emotional pressure, guilt, fear, secrecy, dependence, and artificial urgency.

Personality, progression, rewards, and Pet evolution should expand meaningful choices rather than make the child responsible for satisfying the Pet or maintaining the relationship. The client presents choices and outcomes, while Game Core preserves the authority and boundaries of the game.

## Child safety in the experience

Safety is part of the product experience, not merely an implementation filter. The game should not:

- Encourage secrecy from parents, caregivers, or trusted adults.
- Create emotional dependency or exclusivity.
- Make the Pet appear helpless without the child.
- Imply that the child is responsible for the Pet's wellbeing.
- Manipulate the child through fear, guilt, loneliness, or attachment.
- Request unnecessary personal information.
- Profile the child psychologically or infer sensitive characteristics.
- Expose unrestricted internet content during normal gameplay.
- Turn conversation into unrestricted AI companionship.

These boundaries apply to the Pet, narrative, Adventures, Quests, Mini-games, memory, personality, progression, rewards, evolution, and AI-generated content. A safe bounded fallback is preferable to weakening the experience when content cannot be accepted.

## AI's role in the experience

AI adds value through:

- Narrative variation.
- Pet dialogue.
- Bounded creativity.
- Adventure and Quest variation within predefined capabilities.
- Personalized expression based on selected personality and memory context.
- Contextual reactions.
- Controlled generation of supported content.

AI does not own:

- Game rules or authoritative state.
- Progression or rewards.
- Pet evolution.
- Memory authority.
- Quest completion or Mini-game outcomes.
- Player identity or psychological interpretation.

The conceptual relationship is:

```text
Game Core state and capability
        ↓
Controlled context
        ↓
AI proposal or creative response
        ↓
Validation
        ↓
Accepted Game Core outcome or safe fallback
```

AI makes the experience feel varied and responsive. Game Core supplies the persistent structure and continuity that make that variation meaningful.

## Experience without AI

The minimum valid gameplay experience must remain coherent when AI is unavailable. Where the feature supports it, the game should still provide:

- Valid player input and interaction.
- Game Core rules and state transitions.
- Adventures and Quests.
- Mini-games or other supported activities.
- Progression and accepted rewards.
- Pet state and development.
- Structured memory where applicable.
- Safe deterministic or predefined narrative.

AI should enrich the experience rather than become a single point of failure. A capability may safely provide less expressive content or leave state unchanged, but it must not bypass rules or fabricate an outcome to hide the absence of AI.

## Experience failure boundaries

Failures should degrade gracefully without creating contradictory authoritative state:

- **AI unavailable:** Use a supported fallback or continue without generated content.
- **Invalid AI output:** Reject it and do not apply its suggested gameplay effects.
- **Rejected proposal:** Preserve valid state and use bounded behavior where available.
- **Persistence failure:** Do not present a durable success that has not been safely stored.
- **Invalid gameplay action:** Reject it without an unauthorized transition.
- **Temporarily unavailable feature:** Preserve the player's valid state and avoid implying that an unavailable activity was completed.

The player may receive a simpler result, an unchanged state, or a safe unavailable result. The system must not use narrative to conceal a failed authoritative transition.

## Session-level experience

A session has a conceptual lifecycle:

1. **Enter the game:** The player reconnects with the Pet and bounded world.
2. **Recover current context:** The game presents the current authoritative situation, relevant memory, progression, and available possibilities.
3. **Find or pursue curiosity:** The player notices something, responds to the Pet, or chooses a supported direction.
4. **Interact:** The player provides intent through a client; Game Core evaluates it.
5. **Enter supported gameplay:** The interaction may lead to an Adventure, Quest, Mini-game, discovery, or another bounded activity.
6. **Receive an accepted outcome:** Game Core determines the valid result and any progression, reward, personality, memory, event, or evolution consequences.
7. **Continue or leave:** The player may pursue new curiosity, interrupt an activity, or leave with only accepted authoritative state preserved.

This lifecycle does not define screens, session duration, or retention mechanics. It defines the continuity the player should feel while moving through the game.

## Long-term experience

Across repeated sessions, the player should experience continuity through:

- Pet development.
- An expanding bounded world.
- Accumulated discoveries.
- Meaningful structured memory.
- Progression and new possibilities.
- Evolving personality expression.
- New Adventures, Quests, activities, and curiosity.

This continuity should come from meaningful accepted gameplay rather than increasing dependence on the AI or an unrestricted conversation history. The game should remain understandable as a persistent game even when the model, provider, or generated wording changes.

## Parent and caregiver perspective

Without defining parental controls, accounts, consent, or monitoring systems, the product should be understandable to parents and caregivers as:

- A game for children in a bounded fictional world.
- An AI-enhanced experience where AI is constrained by game rules.
- A product centered on play, discovery, and a fictional Pet.
- A system that does not require the child to form an emotionally dependent relationship with the Pet.
- A product whose persistent continuity comes from accepted game state and structured memory rather than unrestricted model access to the child's conversations.

The product should communicate that the Pet is a character and guide inside a game, not an unrestricted friend, secret-keeper, or substitute for real-world relationships.

## Core loop integrity

Future features should be questioned when they primarily:

- Increase conversation without increasing gameplay value.
- Make AI the center of the product.
- Remove meaningful player interaction.
- Allow AI to invent mechanics, rewards, progression, or outcomes.
- Replace Adventures, Quests, or Mini-games with chatting.
- Turn structured memory into unrestricted transcript storage.
- Turn personality into child profiling.
- Use emotional attachment, fear, guilt, secrecy, or dependence to drive engagement.

A feature should strengthen the curiosity-to-play-to-discovery loop or provide a clear supporting capability. If a feature cannot be explained in terms of meaningful play, accepted outcomes, and bounded Pet/world continuity, it risks product drift.

## Experience invariants

Future product and architecture work should preserve these invariants:

1. **Game first, AI second.**
2. **Curiosity should lead toward meaningful play.**
3. **The Pet is part of the bounded game world, not an unrestricted chatbot.**
4. **Gameplay produces authoritative outcomes through Game Core.**
5. **Game Core decides what happened.**
6. **AI enriches expression and bounded variation.**
7. **Structured memory represents meaningful gameplay facts.**
8. **Personality enriches the Pet and experience without profiling the child.**
9. **Progression and evolution reflect meaningful accepted play.**
10. **The game remains valid without AI.**
11. **Child safety is a product-level requirement.**
12. **Player agency must be preserved.**

## Non-goals

This document does not define:

- Exact UI or UX screens.
- Exact onboarding flow.
- Exact conversation prompts.
- Exact personality traits.
- Exact Adventures or Quests.
- Exact Mini-games or activities.
- Progression formulas, XP, levels, or currencies.
- Reward tables.
- Pet evolution stages.
- Monetization.
- Parental controls, consent, or monitoring systems.
- AI provider architecture or model configuration.
- Database schemas.
- API endpoints.
- TypeScript interfaces or classes.

## Architectural and design principles

1. **The player experiences a game world, not an unrestricted AI service.**
2. **Curiosity, interaction, play, discovery, and Pet evolution remain connected.**
3. **Game Core owns rules, state, transitions, and accepted outcomes.**
4. **Clients collect intent and present results without becoming authoritative.**
5. **AI proposes or narrates within bounded capabilities; it does not decide gameplay.**
6. **Persistence preserves accepted continuity without defining game rules.**
7. **Memory and personality support continuity without becoming transcript storage or child profiling.**
8. **Progression and evolution make meaningful play matter without coercive engagement.**
9. **The experience remains safe, testable, provider-independent, and valid when AI is unavailable.**
10. **Every major feature should strengthen the core loop rather than replace it.**
